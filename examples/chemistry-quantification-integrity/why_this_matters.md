# Why this concern matters

The scientific issue is measurement validity, not presentation style.

An HPLC-UV detector measures absorbance response. The integrated signal depends on how strongly each chemical species absorbs at the selected wavelength, not only on how many moles are present.

For a single analyte with a validated calibration curve, HPLC-UV can be strongly quantitative. The problem here is different: the manuscript compares many chemically distinct products using raw area fractions as though every species had the same response factor.

A useful chemistry reviewer therefore asks:

- What physical quantity did the instrument actually measure?
- What transformation converts that signal into the claimed quantity?
- Has that transformation been calibrated across the chemical diversity used to support the conclusion?

The revision path is concrete: use isolated yields or a validated quantitative analytical method, then reassess whether the “uniformly high yielding” claim survives.
