# Curated reference review

## Major concern — raw HPLC-UV area fraction is being treated as quantitative molar yield across chemically different products

**Claim under review**

The manuscript uses 78–96% “yields” across 24 substrates to support broad functional-group tolerance and uniformly high synthetic efficiency.

**Evidence anchor**

The Methods define yield as product peak area divided by total integrated HPLC area at 254 nm. The scope contains products with substantially different aromatic and heteroaromatic chromophores, but no response-factor calibration or authentic-standard quantification is reported.

**Failure mode**

UV absorbance response is compound dependent. A chromatographic area fraction at one wavelength is not generally equivalent to molar fraction or reaction yield when products, substrates, by-products, and impurities have different extinction coefficients. The problem becomes more important when the scope intentionally spans compounds with different conjugation and heteroaromatic substitution.

**Why this matters**

The quantitative values are not merely descriptive. They are the main evidence for the paper's claim that the reaction is consistently high yielding across chemical space. If detector response varies substantially among products, both the absolute yields and the apparent uniformity of the scope can be distorted.

**Alternative explanation**

Some “high-yielding” entries may primarily have strong 254 nm response, whereas lower-response products or impurities may be underrepresented in the area-normalized calculation.

**Required revision**

The authors should replace raw area percentages with a defensible quantitative method. Suitable options include:

1. isolated yields for the preparatively relevant scope entries;
2. calibrated HPLC/UPLC quantification using authentic standards and compound-specific response factors;
3. quantitative NMR with a validated internal standard where appropriate; or
4. a justified relative-response approach with validation showing that response-factor variation does not alter the scope conclusions.

The manuscript should also distinguish **assay yield**, **conversion**, **selectivity**, and **isolated yield** rather than using “yield” interchangeably.

**Severity:** Major

**Calibrated conclusion before re-quantification**

The current chromatograms may support product formation across the tested substrate set, but they do not by themselves establish uniformly high quantitative yields.
