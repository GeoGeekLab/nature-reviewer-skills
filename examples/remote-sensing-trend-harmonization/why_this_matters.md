# Why this concern matters

This is not a request for “more validation” in the abstract. It is a specific identifiability problem.

The manuscript has two changes occurring at the same time:

- the quantity of scientific interest may change; and
- the measurement system definitely changes.

When those two transitions coincide, a statistical breakpoint alone cannot identify which transition generated the discontinuity.

A scientifically useful reviewer therefore does three things:

1. **anchors the concern** to the sensor transition and the detected breakpoint;
2. **states the competing explanation** — an observing-system artifact; and
3. **asks for discriminating evidence** — overlap harmonization, same-sensor sensitivity, explicit sensor terms, and uncertainty propagation.

The claim can still survive, but only if the trend is robust to the measurement-system transition. If that evidence is unavailable, the scientifically appropriate revision is to narrow the conclusion.
