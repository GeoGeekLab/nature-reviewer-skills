# Why this concern matters

“Autonomous” is not a synonym for “contains automation.”

For a closed-loop engineering system, the system boundary includes the operations required to keep the loop functioning:

- execution;
- failure detection;
- recovery;
- measurement acceptance/rejection;
- maintenance;
- restart logic; and
- return of a valid observation to the optimizer.

If a person performs those functions, the scientific question is not whether the platform is useful—it may be very useful. The question is whether **full autonomy** is the correct description of the demonstrated system.

A strong reviewer therefore avoids a vague objection such as “more real-world testing is needed.” Instead, the reviewer identifies exactly which human decisions sit inside the causal loop and asks the authors to quantify or automate them.

The resulting revision can be either technical or semantic:

- automate the missing recovery and quality-control functions; or
- narrow the claim to accurately describe a human-supervised automated platform.
