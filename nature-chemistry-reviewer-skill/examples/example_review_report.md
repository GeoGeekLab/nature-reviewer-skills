Reviewer Reports on the Initial Version

Referees' comments:

Referee #1 (Remarks to the Author):

The manuscript presents a potentially useful catalytic transformation and the synthetic motivation is clear. The central issue is that the strongest claims about generality and mechanistic origin are broader than the current evidence.

Major comments:

1. The novelty claim should be recalibrated against the closest reaction families and catalyst systems. The revision should state precisely whether the advance is a new transformation, a new selectivity pattern, a broader scope, or a mechanistic insight.
2. The substrate scope does not yet test the electronic, steric, and functional-group boundaries implied by the main text. A revised scope table should include representative failed or low-yielding substrates and explain the chemical basis of the limitations.
3. The proposed mechanism is plausible but not uniquely established. The authors should add discriminating experiments or temper the mechanistic language.

Specific comments:

1. Define the selectivity metric consistently across the main text and supplementary tables.
2. Provide calibration and uncertainty information for product quantification.

Overall judgment:

The work could become suitable after substantial revision that narrows the claims and strengthens the evidence chain.

Referee #2 (Remarks to the Author):

The experimental study is carefully motivated, but the chemical identity and performance claims require a more complete audit.

Major comments:

1. Key intermediates and products require clearer orthogonal characterization. The authors should provide source spectra and purity metrics for compounds supporting the central conclusion.
2. The control set does not fully exclude background reactivity. Appropriate no-catalyst, inactive-analogue, and matched-loading controls should be added where feasible.
3. Activity comparisons should be normalized using chemically meaningful denominators so that loading, morphology, or concentration effects are not conflated with intrinsic activity.

Specific comments:

1. Report replicate numbers and whether error bars represent technical or independent synthetic replicates.
2. Align figure labels with the exact reaction conditions used in the supplementary methods.

Overall judgment:

The manuscript is promising but requires stronger validation before the main chemical claims are secure.

Referee #3 (Remarks to the Author):

The manuscript would benefit from improved reproducibility and data transparency.

Major comments:

1. The experimental procedure omits several details needed for reproduction, including atmosphere control, timing, and purification conditions. These should be reported in a consolidated table.
2. The statistical treatment is too limited for comparisons that drive the conclusions. Confidence intervals or replicate-level data should be provided for key measurements.
3. Source data, spectra, chromatograms, and computational inputs should be deposited or provided as supplementary files.

Specific comments:

1. Use consistent compound labels across schemes, figures, and supplementary tables.
2. Avoid terms such as robust and general unless quantitative thresholds are given.

Overall judgment:

The paper requires revision focused on reproducibility, uncertainty, and claim precision.
