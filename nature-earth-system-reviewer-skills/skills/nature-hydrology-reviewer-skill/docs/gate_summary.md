# Hydrology gate summary

## Claim-evidence calibration gate

Check whether hydrological claims exceed the demonstrated evidence. Separate observation, inference, mechanism, attribution and application. Require weaker wording or additional analyses when claims depend on untested assumptions.

## Hydrological variable validity gate

Check whether the measured or modelled variable can support the claim: stage versus discharge, TWS versus groundwater, precipitation anomaly versus drought impact, flood extent versus hazard, concentration versus load, surface area versus storage.

## Scale, resolution and basin consistency gate

Check whether process scale, grid resolution, basin boundaries, river reaches, aquifer systems and administrative units are consistent. Hydrological inference should respect drainage, connectivity and upstream/downstream structure.

## Validation and gauge representativeness gate

Check whether validation is independent and representative across basins, hydroclimates, flow regimes, terrain, river sizes and human regulation. Watch for data-rich-region bias and ungauged-basin overclaiming.

## Trend and attribution gate

Check time-series length, baseline choice, variability, confounders and attribution design. Short records often support recent variability, not climate-change attribution.

## Human-water interaction gate

Check whether reservoirs, dams, pumping, irrigation, withdrawals, transfers, land-use change and policy rules are separated from natural hydroclimatic variation.

## Hydrological model assumption gate

Check forcing data, model structure, calibration, routing, groundwater representation, reservoir/irrigation modules, ensembles, scenarios and sensitivity tests.

## Extreme-event definition gate

Check thresholds, return periods, baselines, duration, seasonality, antecedent state, compound dependence and non-stationarity for flood, drought and other water hazards.

## Water-quality transport gate

Check whether concentration, load, flux, source, pathway, residence time and event sampling are distinguished when water quality or solute transport is used.

## Remote sensing and data assimilation gate

Check sensor/algorithm consistency, retrieval limits, quality flags, masks, spatial leakage, assimilated-data dependence and downstream hydrological indicator validation.

## Statistical inference and uncertainty gate

Check autocorrelation, multiple testing, field significance, threshold sensitivity, uncertainty propagation, performance metrics and non-stationarity.

## Reproducibility, data and code gate

Check whether basin boundaries, station IDs, masks, product versions, quality flags, model configs, forcing data, scenario files, splits, seeds and scripts are sufficient to reproduce main analyses.

## Novelty and related-work positioning gate

Check whether novelty is defined against closest hydrology products, models, datasets, methods and impact studies. Separate technical novelty from hydrological knowledge gain.

## Policy and management interpretation gate

Check whether management conclusions are bounded by hydrological reliability, decision scale, governance assumptions and uncertainty.

## Detail audit gate

Check definitions, units, variable names, thresholds, figure captions, references, supplementary traceability and availability statements.

## Referee voice and output format gate

A Nature-style referee report is not a checklist. It recognizes contribution, identifies central evidence-chain risk, gives issue-reason-revision guidance, separates major from minor concerns and avoids exposing internal diagnostic labels.


## Classic hydrology stress-test gate

Purpose: use recurring issues from high-impact Nature/Science hydrology debates as low-weight contextual calibration.

Apply only when the manuscript itself makes a matching claim. Do not elevate severity merely because a topic resembles a classic debate.

Main axes: non-stationarity, freshwater-boundary scaling, composite threat indices, drought metric dependence, flood-risk chain separation, virtual-water depletion accounting, and basin-management attribution under warming.


## Close-reading discipline

Detail audit should operate at figure-level, table-level and method-level when the manuscript evidence is available. The reviewer may use short exact manuscript quotations in bold to calibrate claim wording, but should not quote long passages or invent anchors. Novelty review should compare the manuscript with the closest products, models, datasets, regional inventories and impact studies, distinguishing product novelty from hydrological knowledge gain. These controls guide output quality without changing the evidence weight of the primary reviewer-memory gates.
