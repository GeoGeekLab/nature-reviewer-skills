# Classic hydrology stress-test gate

This reference converts high-impact Nature and Science hydrology debates into abstract review stress tests. It does not store source text, reviewer reports, or author responses. It is a contextual calibration layer: it can sharpen an already-triggered concern, but it must not increase severity or override the peer-review-file-derived reviewer memory by itself.

Preferred internal pattern:

```text
Claim type -> classic controversy risk -> reviewer concern -> revision direction
```

## Non-weighting rule

Use these stress tests only when the submitted manuscript makes a matching claim. A classic controversy should not be cited as decorative background and should not create an additional major concern unless the manuscript-specific evidence chain is weak.

## Stress-test axes

| Axis | Internal reviewer use |
|---|---|
| Non-stationarity and water management | Test whether historical baselines, design rules, return periods or adaptation claims are justified under changing climate and land/water use. |
| Global freshwater boundaries | Test whether global thresholds are translated responsibly to basin, aquifer, green-water and blue-water contexts. |
| Integrated water-security threat indices | Test whether composite maps separate stressor weighting, infrastructure mitigation, ecological consequence and human water-security interpretation. |
| Drought-index controversy | Test whether drought claims depend on a single metric, evaporative-demand formulation or meteorological proxy rather than hydrological response. |
| Global flood-risk projection | Test whether hazard, exposure, vulnerability, protection standards and climate-model spread are separated. |
| Groundwater depletion in food trade | Test whether embodied-water or virtual-water claims distinguish depletion, withdrawal, recharge, irrigation source and trade allocation assumptions. |
| Basin management under warming | Test whether temperature-driven runoff loss, precipitation variability, reservoir operation and demand assumptions are decomposed before policy conclusions. |

## Source anchors

The memory layer is based on public bibliographic information and abstract controversy structure from high-impact Nature/Science literature, including Milly et al. (Science, 2008), Rockström et al. (Nature, 2009), Vörösmarty et al. (Nature, 2010), Steffen et al. (Science, 2015) and associated Comment/Response, Hirabayashi et al. (Nature Climate Change, 2013), Dai (Nature Climate Change, 2013), Dalin et al. (Nature, 2017), and Milly & Dunne (Science, 2020).

Do not expose this gate name or source list in final referee reports unless the user explicitly asks for diagnostic provenance.
