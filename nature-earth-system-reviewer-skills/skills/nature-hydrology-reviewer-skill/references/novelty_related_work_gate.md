# Novelty and related-work positioning gate

Check whether novelty is defined against the closest hydrology products, models, datasets, regional inventories, observation networks, forecast systems, attribution studies and impact assessments. Separate technical novelty from hydrological knowledge gain.

A strong review should ask what is new at the claim level:

- new variable, new process insight, new causal evidence, new prediction skill or new decision-relevant evidence;
- broader spatial coverage, finer resolution, longer record, improved validation or merely different processing;
- better performance in difficult basins, aquifers, regimes or events, not only in globally averaged metrics;
- clearer uncertainty, reproducibility or transferability than previous work;
- changed scientific conclusion or management interpretation relative to the closest existing work.

When the manuscript claims broad novelty, require comparison with the closest existing work in terms of domain, resolution, validation, uncertainty and hydrological interpretation. If the advance is mainly a product, map or database, ask whether it produces new hydrological inference or mainly enables future work.

Preferred internal pattern:

```text
Claim type -> evidence risk -> reviewer concern -> revision direction
```

This gate should be applied only when the manuscript's claims require it. Do not expose gate names or pattern IDs in final referee reports.
