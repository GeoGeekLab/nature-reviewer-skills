Reviewer Reports on the Initial Version

Referees' comments:

Referee #1 (Remarks to the Author)

The manuscript presents a potentially valuable basin-scale assessment of recent river-water-level variability using satellite altimetry. The broad spatial coverage and emphasis on data-sparse regions are important. However, the central interpretation is stronger than the demonstrated evidence because the manuscript treats recent water-level variability as a direct indicator of discharge change and long-term climate-driven hydrological change.

Major comments:

1. The manuscript needs to separate river stage, discharge and water availability. If the authors intend to draw conclusions about discharge or basin water resources, they should provide rating-curve evidence, independent discharge validation or a narrower interpretation focused on water-level variability.
2. The wording **"global changes in river discharge"** is not supported by water-level retrievals alone unless channel geometry, hydraulic controls and rating relationships are addressed. The authors should either validate discharge inference or revise the claim to water-level variability.
3. Validation coverage should be reported by river size, hydroclimate, terrain and regulation status. A global error statement is not justified if validation is concentrated in data-rich regions.

Specific comments:

- In Fig. 2, please distinguish sites with gauge-based validation from sites with only satellite-derived estimates. This would help readers judge where the map is observation-supported versus extrapolated.
- Could the authors clarify whether the trend baseline is hydrologically comparable across monsoon, snowmelt and arid basins?
- Table 1 should report sample size and error statistics by basin regime rather than only global summary metrics.
- Define the minimum-observation threshold and show whether the main spatial pattern remains under alternative thresholds.

Overall judgment:

The work is promising, but the main claims require substantial recalibration and additional validation before publication-level support is achieved.

Referee #2 (Remarks to the Author)

This study addresses an important hydrological observation problem and the manuscript is clearly within the scope of a high-impact water-cycle contribution. My main concern is not the relevance of the dataset, but whether the validation design is sufficient for the level of generalization made across basins and hydroclimatic regimes.

Major comments:

1. The validation should be independent of product construction, calibration and screening decisions. The manuscript should state which stations, reaches or basins were used during algorithm development and which were held out for evaluation.
2. The methods should allow the reader to reproduce the figures. Please provide station identifiers, masks, quality-flag filters, product versions, aggregation windows, random seeds and scripts for the reach-level workflow.
3. Uncertainty propagation is not yet adequate. Retrieval error, sampling frequency, missing observations, spatial leakage and temporal aggregation should be carried into the trend and regional summary results.

Specific comments:

- The Methods section should specify whether reservoir-regulated reaches were included, excluded or analyzed separately.
- Could the authors provide a table linking each main figure to the input datasets, filtering criteria and uncertainty estimate used to create it?
- The phrase **"robust across global rivers"** should be reserved for results supported by regime-stratified or basin-holdout validation.
- The data availability statement should indicate whether processed reach-level products are sufficient to reproduce all map panels and summary statistics.

Overall judgment:

The manuscript has a strong observational premise, but the validation and uncertainty treatment need substantial strengthening before the global conclusions are reliable.

Referee #3 (Remarks to the Author)

The paper has the potential to make a useful contribution by extending hydrological monitoring into regions where in-situ measurements are sparse. However, the manuscript currently mixes product novelty, hydrological interpretation and management relevance in a way that makes the contribution appear broader than the evidence supports.

Major comments:

1. The novelty should be positioned against the closest existing altimetry, gauge, reanalysis and hydrological-model products. The manuscript should specify whether the advance is spatial coverage, temporal frequency, retrieval method, validation, or new hydrological inference.
2. Human-water interactions need clearer treatment. Reservoir operations, withdrawals, irrigation, channel modification and water transfers can alter river level independently of climate-driven hydrological change.
3. The management implications should be bounded. Claims about water availability, risk or planning should be stated only where the measured variable, validation and uncertainty are adequate for those interpretations.

Specific comments:

- Please add a concise comparison table against the closest existing products, including variable measured, spatial resolution, temporal coverage, validation basis and main limitation.
- In the caption of Fig. 4, distinguish observed water-level change from inferred discharge, storage or scarcity.
- Could the authors clarify what new hydrological conclusion follows from the product beyond improved coverage?
- Avoid using “risk”, “availability” or “scarcity” unless the corresponding hazard, demand or vulnerability terms are explicitly analyzed.

Overall judgment:

I would view this as a potentially important dataset and monitoring paper, but the interpretation should be narrowed and the human-water confounding and related-work positioning improved.
