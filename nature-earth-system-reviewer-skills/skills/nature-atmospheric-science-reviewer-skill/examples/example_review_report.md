# Reviewer Reports on the Initial Version

Referees' comments:

## Referee #1 (Remarks to the Author)

The manuscript addresses an important atmospheric-science problem and presents a potentially useful synthesis of observations and modelling. The central result would be of broad interest if the authors can show that the claimed mechanism is not an artefact of product choice, domain selection, or model configuration.

Major comments:
1. The abstract and conclusions state the mechanism more generally than the evidence supports. This matters because the analysis demonstrates a robust association within the selected period and domain, but does not yet establish that the same mechanism operates across seasons, circulation regimes, and alternative observational products. The authors should narrow the headline claim or add product-comparison, regime-stratified, and baseline-sensitivity analyses.
2. The manuscript should distinguish the novelty of the dataset, diagnostic, or model configuration from the novelty of the atmospheric interpretation. This distinction is important because a new analysis workflow does not automatically establish a new physical mechanism. The revision should position the result against the closest observational products, model intercomparisons, and process studies.
3. The broader implications should be tied to the demonstrated evidence chain. If the authors intend to make a global or forcing-level claim, they should show how uncertainty propagates from the observed or simulated variable to the implied atmospheric response.

Specific comments:
- Define the event threshold, baseline period, product version, and sample size in the main text and figure captions.
- Clarify whether the key result is sensitive to season, circulation regime, and alternative product screening.

Overall judgment:
The manuscript is potentially suitable for a broad Nature-family audience after substantial revision that recalibrates the claims and strengthens cross-regime support.

## Referee #2 (Remarks to the Author)

The methods are promising, but the validation design and uncertainty hierarchy need strengthening. The manuscript uses several atmospheric products and model outputs, yet the degree of independence among these constraints is not always clear.

Major comments:
1. Reanalysis and satellite products are treated as near-independent constraints. This matters because several diagnostics may share forcing, assimilation, retrieval assumptions, or quality-control filters. The authors should identify which datasets are independent, quantify product-specific uncertainty, and propagate these uncertainties into the main figures.
2. The model evaluation should be aligned with the process claim. Aggregate skill metrics are insufficient if the conclusion depends on boundary-layer structure, cloud response, chemistry, or circulation anomalies. The authors should add process-level diagnostics and sensitivity tests for the configuration choices that control the main result.
3. The uncertainty shown for the headline conclusion appears to emphasize statistical fit more than product, sampling, and structural uncertainty. The authors should separate these components and show which source dominates the final interpretation.

Specific comments:
- Report the effective sample size where temporal persistence or spatial dependence is substantial.
- Archive the analysis scripts, product versions, preprocessing filters, and source data needed to reproduce the main figures.

Overall judgment:
The manuscript is technically interesting, but the validation and uncertainty treatment should be made more transparent before the main quantitative claim is persuasive.

## Referee #3 (Remarks to the Author)

The proposed physical explanation is plausible and scientifically relevant, but it is not yet fully closed. The manuscript should more clearly separate correlation, mechanism diagnosis, and causal attribution.

Major comments:
1. The mechanism is described in causal terms, but the pathway is not completely quantified. This matters because circulation, moisture, energy, aerosol, chemical, or radiative budget terms may offer alternative explanations. The authors should close the relevant budget or provide targeted sensitivity tests.
2. Internal variability and known modes of atmospheric variability are not yet treated as central confounders. This is important because the inferred mechanism may depend on the selected period or background circulation state. The revision should test the role of relevant modes, baseline choice, and ensemble spread.
3. Several figure-level definitions are essential to interpretation. If thresholds, levels, masks, or filters change the result, those choices should be visible in the main figures and methods rather than only in supplementary material.

Specific comments:
- Clarify sign conventions, vertical levels, units, and reference periods consistently across text and figures.
- State whether alternative domain boundaries or aggregation windows materially change the main conclusion.

Overall judgment:
The paper has clear potential, but the causal interpretation and figure-level audit trail require substantial revision.
