# Atmospheric science gates

Each gate follows: claim type → evidence risk → reviewer concern → revision direction.

## Product validity

Observation or product claim → product-specific bias is untreated → reviewer questions whether the atmospheric variable is actually measured at the claimed fidelity → add independent validation, product intercomparison, uncertainty, and regime-specific screening.

## Model physics

Model-based mechanism claim → physical parameterization or forcing choice controls the result → reviewer questions whether the mechanism is robust or model-imposed → add sensitivity tests, ensemble evidence, or claim limitation.

## Scale consistency

Regional or process claim → grid, footprint, vertical level, or time aggregation mismatches the claimed process → reviewer questions whether the phenomenon is resolved → align scale or narrow the claim.

## AI fidelity

AI model claim → skill metrics look strong but physical consistency is untested → reviewer questions weather/climate credibility outside benchmark scores → add physical diagnostics, OOD tests, long-rollout stability, and fair baselines.

## Aerosol-cloud-radiation

Forcing or cloud response claim → aerosol, meteorology, cloud regime, and radiative adjustment are conflated → reviewer questions causal isolation → decompose pathways and propagate uncertainty.

## Chemistry and emissions budget

Net climate or chemistry claim → inventory, lifetime, transformation, transport, deposition, or co-emissions are weakly constrained → reviewer questions budget closure → add source/sink diagnostics and alternative inventory sensitivity.

## Attribution and extremes

Event attribution claim → event definition, counterfactual forcing, or tail extrapolation controls the result → reviewer questions attribution strength → test definitions, evaluate models for the event class, and quantify return-period uncertainty.

## Trend and variability

Trend claim → short records, autocorrelation, product choice, or endpoint sensitivity drive significance → reviewer questions forced-signal detection → test internal variability, effective sample size, baseline, and product/ensemble agreement.

## Mechanism and causality

Mechanistic claim → correlation or composite is treated as causal explanation → reviewer questions alternative pathways → add process closure, perturbation tests, lag analysis, or soften causal language.

## Reproducibility

Complex workflow claim → data and code provenance is insufficient → reviewer cannot assess reproducibility → provide data versions, scripts, configuration, checksums, model weights where relevant, and workflow documentation.
