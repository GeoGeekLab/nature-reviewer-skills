# Ice Sheet Glacier Dynamics

Grounding lines, calving, ocean access, geometry uncertainty, buttressing, inventories and tipping terminology.

## Internal questions

- **Grounding-line location uncertainty omitted:** Grounding-line migration or retreat is central, but the mapping method, tidal state, hydrostatic assumption, and positional uncertainty are not quantified. Resolution: Describe the detection method, provide uncertainty and tidal sensitivity, and compare independent observations where available.
- **Calving attribution ignores geometry and stress history:** A calving event is attributed to climate forcing without considering rift history, pinning points, mélange, geometry, and preconditioning. Resolution: Reconstruct stress and geometry evolution, test alternative triggers, and distinguish event timing from long-term forcing.
- **Ocean-temperature metric is not dynamically relevant:** Offshore, depth-averaged, or sparse temperature is used as the forcing for glacier or ice-shelf melt without demonstrating access to the ice interface. Resolution: Use cavity or fjord-relevant thermal forcing, demonstrate pathways, and quantify lag and transport uncertainty.
- **Bed or bathymetry uncertainty is underexplored:** Conclusions about stability, retreat, or melt depend on poorly resolved bed or seafloor geometry. Resolution: Propagate alternative bed/bathymetry realizations and identify conclusions that remain robust.
- **Dynamic and climatic mass loss are not separated:** Observed retreat or thinning is attributed to atmospheric or oceanic forcing without separating dynamic adjustment, calving, surface balance, and basal melt. Resolution: Partition components using independent observations or models and calibrate attribution to what can be resolved.
- **Ice-shelf buttressing claim lacks stress evidence:** Loss or thinning of floating ice is assumed to increase grounded-ice discharge without quantifying buttressing or stress transfer. Resolution: Calculate buttressing or stress sensitivity and identify the grounded catchments dynamically connected to the change.
- **Glacier inventory changes through time:** Trend analysis mixes glacier outlines, merges/splits, surge states, or inclusion criteria across epochs. Resolution: Use a stable inventory or document lineage, treat surge-type glaciers separately, and quantify mapping uncertainty.
- **Tipping language lacks state-space evidence:** Abrupt past or modeled change is labeled a tipping point without demonstrating multiple states, hysteresis, threshold behavior, or self-sustaining change. Resolution: Define the tipping criterion, test hysteresis or recovery, and use calibrated language if only abrupt change is established.

Use only when relevant to a central claim. Do not expose this gate name or pattern IDs in the final referee report.
