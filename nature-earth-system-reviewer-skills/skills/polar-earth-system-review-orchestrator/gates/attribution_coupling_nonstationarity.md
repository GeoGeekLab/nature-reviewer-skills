# Attribution Coupling Nonstationarity

Causality, internal variability, feedback direction, teleconnections, lags, counterfactuals and changing relationships.

## Internal questions

- **Correlation is promoted to mechanism:** Covariation between sea ice, circulation, ocean heat, ecology, or greenhouse-gas flux is described as a causal process without discriminating evidence. Resolution: Specify causal alternatives, use temporal or intervention-like evidence where possible, and calibrate language to association unless alternatives are excluded.
- **Internal variability is underrepresented:** A short trend or extreme event is attributed to external forcing without estimating variability from observations, large ensembles, or physically appropriate null models. Resolution: Quantify the expected distribution under internal variability, test sensitivity to trend window, and express attribution probabilistically.
- **Feedback direction is not identified:** Coupled variables are interpreted as a one-way driver-response relation although reciprocal feedbacks are plausible. Resolution: Use lagged/process evidence, perturbation experiments, or budget analysis to distinguish direction; otherwise present a coupled association.
- **Teleconnection pathway is incomplete:** A remote climate mode is linked to a polar response without closing the dynamical pathway or testing competing modes and seasonality. Resolution: Demonstrate the circulation pathway, test multivariate and detrended alternatives, and evaluate stability across periods or models.
- **Historical relationship assumed stationary:** An empirical climate, ecological, or proxy relationship is extrapolated into a regime with changing ice cover, stratification, species composition, or atmospheric moisture. Resolution: Test time-varying parameters or regime interactions, diagnose extrapolation, and restrict forecasts to supported conditions.
- **Process timing is inconsistent with causation:** The proposed driver does not precede the response at a physically plausible lag or the analysis averages away the relevant sequence. Resolution: Analyze event chronology and lags, compare alternative pathways, and revise the mechanism if timing is inconsistent.
- **Counterfactual is not defined:** Terms such as anthropogenic contribution, policy benefit, tipping avoidance, or avoided warming are used without an explicit counterfactual experiment. Resolution: Define the counterfactual, justify forcing differences, quantify uncertainty, and separate scenario comparison from formal attribution.
- **Mechanism depends on one diagnostic:** A broad process conclusion rests on a single index, composite, regression coefficient, or model diagnostic. Resolution: Add convergent process evidence, test alternative diagnostics, and state which mechanism components remain inferred.

Use only when relevant to a central claim. Do not expose this gate name or pattern IDs in the final referee report.
