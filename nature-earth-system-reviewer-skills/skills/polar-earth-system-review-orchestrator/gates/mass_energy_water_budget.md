# Mass Energy Water Budget

Conservation, control volumes, firn/density conversion, snow assumptions, freshwater routing, energy and carbon closure.

## Internal questions

- **Budget does not close:** The central mass, heat, freshwater, carbon, or energy budget omits material terms or leaves an unexplained residual. Resolution: Define the control volume and sign convention, report every term and residual, and propagate uncertainty into closure.
- **Grounded and floating ice are mixed:** Mass or melt changes on ice shelves and floating tongues are combined with grounded-ice loss when discussing sea-level contribution. Resolution: Separate grounded and floating domains, state grounding-line masks, and quantify dynamic effects rather than implying direct equivalence.
- **Firn and density assumptions dominate conversion:** Volume or height changes are converted to mass with fixed density or insufficient firn treatment. Resolution: Use a validated firn/density model, show sensitivity and uncertainty, and compare against an independent gravimetric or input-output estimate.
- **Snow depth uncertainty omitted from sea-ice thickness:** Freeboard-derived thickness is reported without propagating snow depth, snow density, ice density, and hydrostatic assumptions. Resolution: Report the full uncertainty chain, test alternative snow products, and avoid overprecise volume trends.
- **Freshwater routing lacks travel-time treatment:** Melt or subglacial discharge is linked to downstream ocean, ice-shelf, or ecosystem response without resolving storage, routing, mixing, and lag. Resolution: Constrain travel times and pathways with observations or process models and test alternative timing assumptions.
- **Surface energy terms are incompletely constrained:** Melt or freeze is attributed to one flux while radiation, turbulent fluxes, rain heat, ocean heat, albedo, or conductive storage are omitted or weakly observed. Resolution: Close the energy budget at the relevant temporal scale, report flux uncertainty, and test whether the proposed term explains residual variability.
- **Carbon inventory and flux are confused:** Concentration, inventory, production, oxidation, evasion, and net ecosystem or ocean-atmosphere flux are interpreted interchangeably. Resolution: Separate stocks and gross/net fluxes, define the integration domain and period, and support source-sink attribution with a closed budget.
- **Unresolved basal melt parameterization controls result:** Ice-shelf or ice-sheet response is driven by prescribed basal melt without showing sensitivity to calibration, geometry, cavity circulation, or climate forcing. Resolution: Report calibration data and parameter ranges, run structural alternatives, and separate robust conclusions from parameterization-dependent outcomes.
