# Paleoclimate Proxy Chronology

Age models, calibration range, archive alteration, resolution, multi-proxy evidence, synchronization and analogues.

## Internal questions

- **Age uncertainty is omitted from phase relation:** Lead-lag, synchrony, abruptness, or event duration is interpreted without propagating age-model uncertainty and correlation between dated levels. Resolution: Use age ensembles, propagate correlated age uncertainty, and report whether phase conclusions survive.
- **Proxy calibration is outside its environmental range:** A modern calibration is applied to glacial, interglacial, or polar conditions with different ecology, chemistry, seasonality, or preservation. Resolution: Show calibration coverage, test nonstationarity and alternative proxies, and report extrapolation uncertainty.
- **Post-depositional alteration is underconstrained:** Ice-core isotope, aerosol, gas, snow, or sediment signals are interpreted as direct climate records without quantifying diffusion, wind redistribution, melt, bioturbation, or chemical alteration. Resolution: Model or measure post-depositional processes and state the temporal and amplitude resolution retained by the archive.
- **Sampling resolution cannot resolve claimed event:** The archive sampling interval, smoothing, sedimentation, diffusion, or dating precision is too coarse for the event duration being inferred. Resolution: Quantify the effective resolution, use forward-model tests, and narrow event-scale claims.
- **Single proxy is assigned a unique mechanism:** One geochemical, isotopic, sedimentological, or biological proxy is interpreted as a unique temperature, storm, circulation, or ice-cover signal. Resolution: Use independent proxies and process calibration to discriminate controls; present residual ambiguity explicitly.
- **Core synchronization is circular:** Records are aligned using the same climatic feature whose simultaneity or propagation is then claimed. Resolution: Use independent tie points where possible and repeat the analysis across untuned age ensembles.
- **Past threshold is transferred directly to future:** A paleoclimate transition is used as a quantitative future threshold without accounting for different forcing rates, geometry, boundary conditions, and initial states. Resolution: Separate qualitative analogue evidence from quantitative projection and test transfer with process models.
- **Source area and transport change over time:** A sediment or aerosol proxy assumes a fixed source although fronts, winds, currents, erosion, and catchment routing may shift. Resolution: Use provenance tracers, transport modeling, and sensitivity to source-area change.

Use only when relevant to a central claim. Do not expose this gate name or pattern IDs in the final referee report.
