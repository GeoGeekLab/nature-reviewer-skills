# Regional Scope Seasonality

Operational polar regions, sectors, coordinate systems, seasons, event windows, bipolar transfer and state definitions.

## Internal questions

- **Undefined polar domain:** The manuscript uses labels such as Arctic-wide, Antarctic-wide, high-latitude, or Southern Ocean without an operational spatial boundary aligned to the analysis. Resolution: State the exact mask, coordinate reference, sector definitions, exclusions, and sensitivity to alternative accepted regional boundaries.
- **Season definition mismatched to process:** Calendar seasons are used even though melt, freeze-up, polar night, snow accumulation, or biological timing varies strongly by region and year. Resolution: Use process-based or locally justified seasonal windows, show robustness to reasonable alternatives, and qualify conclusions that depend on the window.
- **Single sector generalized to a hemisphere:** Evidence from one basin, shelf, glacier cluster, station network, or transect is presented as representative of the full Arctic or Antarctic. Resolution: Narrow the claim or add stratified evidence across relevant sectors, explicitly testing spatial heterogeneity.
- **Bipolar analogy lacks transfer argument:** A mechanism established in one hemisphere is transferred to the other without testing differences in land-ocean geometry, seasonality, circulation, or human context. Resolution: Identify the transfer assumptions, test them where data permit, and treat the comparison as a hypothesis unless both systems are evaluated.
- **Coastal and interior regimes conflated:** Coastal, shelf, marginal-ice-zone, interior plateau, and deep-basin observations are pooled despite distinct forcing and response regimes. Resolution: Stratify by regime, report interactions or heterogeneity, and avoid a single polar-wide coefficient when slopes differ.
- **Study period omits critical phase:** The selected interval excludes freeze-up, breakup, winter darkness, shoulder seasons, or an earlier baseline that is necessary to interpret the claimed transition. Resolution: Justify temporal coverage, show the full available record, and test conclusions across alternative start and end dates.
- **Event-year framing mistaken for state change:** An extreme year or short cluster is described as a new climatic or ecological state without a defined state metric or persistence criterion. Resolution: Define the state variable and transition criterion, quantify persistence and recurrence, and compare against an appropriate null or historical distribution.
- **Map projection and cell area not handled:** Area, distance, gradient, or neighborhood calculations are performed in a polar projection without documenting distortion, cell-area weighting, or reprojection effects. Resolution: Document the projection, use area-aware calculations, and demonstrate that reprojection or latitude weighting does not control the result.
