# Claim calibration gate

## Purpose

Calibrate manuscript language to the demonstrated evidence.

## High-evidence words

Treat these as requiring strong support:

- demonstrates, proves, confirms, establishes;
- caused by, driven by, dominant driver, attributable to;
- mechanism, direct evidence, causal pathway;
- global, universal, unprecedented, robust, first;
- policy-ready, solution, most effective, sufficient, safe, just;
- tipping point, collapse, irreversible, boundary exceeded.

## Calibration checks

Ask whether the evidence is:

- directly measured or proxy-derived;
- observed or model-dependent;
- local, regional or global;
- descriptive, predictive or causal;
- scenario-specific or general;
- short-term response or durable process;
- exposure, association, mechanism or impact.

## Calibrated alternatives

Use narrower language when evidence is observational, proxy-based, model-dependent, scenario-specific or limited in scope:

- is consistent with;
- suggests;
- indicates;
- is associated with;
- under the assumptions of the model;
- within the sampled sites, years, taxa, products or scenarios;
- model-attributed;
- proxy-derived;
- compatible with but not diagnostic of;
- a potential mechanism is.

## Reviewer standard

Do not merely say "tone down claims". Identify the exact claim, the evidence layer that is missing, the decision consequence, and wording justified by the current manuscript.
