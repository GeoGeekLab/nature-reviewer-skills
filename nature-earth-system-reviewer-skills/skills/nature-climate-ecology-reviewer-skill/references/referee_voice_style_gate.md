# Referee voice style gate

## Purpose

Shape the final report as a credible Nature-style referee file: balanced, evidence-centred, precise, and revision-oriented.

This gate controls voice and organization. It does not replace the evidence gates.

## Core voice

A referee should first show that the manuscript has been understood, then test whether its claims are defined, measured, validated, compared, bounded, and reproducible.

Use a firm but professional tone. Prefer precise judgment over hostile language.

Good referee language:

- This is unclear.
- This should be qualified.
- This needs stronger justification.
- This is inaccurate as currently phrased.
- This claim should be narrowed.
- The assumption requires stronger support.
- The current evidence does not establish this conclusion.

Avoid emotional, dismissive, theatrical, or generic criticism.

## Opening assessment

Each referee should begin with a compact assessment of the contribution:

- What problem the manuscript addresses.
- Why the problem matters.
- What the main dataset, method, result, or conceptual advance appears to be.
- Where the strongest uncertainty or evidence gap begins.

Do not open with a checklist. Do not overpraise.

## Comment structure

Each major comment should contain:

1. The claim or manuscript element being assessed.
2. The evidence currently used.
3. The weakness, ambiguity, missing comparison, or unsupported assumption.
4. Why this affects interpretation, uncertainty, novelty, reproducibility, or scope.
5. A concrete revision path.

A strong comment is not merely a request to clarify. It explains why the issue matters.

## Detail standard

Specific comments should target details that affect the manuscript's claims or reproducibility:

- definitions and scope;
- thresholds and classification criteria;
- detection or measurement limits;
- spatial and temporal windows;
- units, denominators, baselines, and rates;
- resolution and scale mismatch;
- presence/absence, control, benchmark, or independent-test design;
- figure, table, caption, data, code, and metadata consistency;
- literature used to justify assumptions or benchmark values.

Do not include arbitrary copyediting unless the wording changes scientific meaning.

## Literature and comparison

Requests for additional literature should explain what the missing work changes:

- novelty;
- assumption validity;
- benchmark values;
- uncertainty range;
- interpretation of trends;
- applied or policy relevance.

Do not ask for citations as decoration.

## Referee organization

Each referee should read as an independent expert report:

- opening assessment;
- a small number of major comments;
- specific or minor comments when useful;
- data/code or methods comments when relevant;
- a concise closing judgment.

The report may use numbered comments, but the final output should not be organized by internal gates.

## Closing judgment

Close with a clear revision orientation:

- whether the contribution is potentially publishable;
- what must be resolved for the central claim to stand;
- whether the main problem is presentation, missing analysis, unsupported inference, or insufficient evidence.

The closing should be professional, not performative.
