# Cross-literature stress tests

These stress tests abstract recurring review risks from public debates, comments,
reanalyses and replies in climate and ecology. They are not case summaries. Use them
only when a manuscript makes the same type of claim.

## 1. Nature-based climate mitigation claims

Use when a manuscript claims large mitigation potential from ecosystem restoration,
protection, management or expansion.

Check whether the claim separates stock, flux, uptake rate, permanence, leakage,
baseline, saturation, disturbance, biodiversity trade-offs and implementation limits.

## 2. Ecological classification claims

Use when a manuscript maps or redefines ecosystem types, vegetation classes, land-cover
states or degradation categories.

Check whether definitions, thresholds, spatial grain, seasonality, validation and
category-to-process inference are explicit.

## 3. Resilience and tipping-point claims

Use when a manuscript claims resilience loss, early warning, regime shift, collapse or
critical transition.

Check robustness to proxy choice, window length, detrending, autocorrelation,
non-stationarity, alternative indicators and observational artifacts.

## 4. Dominant-mechanism claims

Use when a manuscript argues that one process controls an ecosystem, biogeochemical or
climate response.

Check whether alternative mechanisms, model structure, parameter identifiability,
confounding and scale mismatch have been addressed.

## 5. Boundary and threshold claims

Use when a manuscript proposes safe limits, thresholds, boundaries or decision cut-offs.

Check metric definition, aggregation logic, uncertainty, sensitivity to threshold
placement, and the separation of biophysical evidence from normative judgement.

## 6. Biodiversity-change claims

Use when a manuscript infers broad biodiversity change from assemblage, monitoring,
trait, abundance or richness data.

Check taxonomic coverage, detectability, sampling effort, spatial bias, temporal bias,
local-to-global interpretation and the distinction between richness, abundance,
composition and loss.

## 7. Observational-product mechanism claims

Use when a manuscript infers ecological or climatic processes from products, proxies,
inventories, databases or reanalysis.

Check validation, product version, cross-product agreement, artifact sensitivity,
measurement-process separation and uncertainty propagation.

## Use policy

Apply these tests through the relevant gate. Do not cite them as authority. Do not turn
them into a fixed checklist. Their role is to calibrate strong claims to the evidence
actually provided.
