# Referee Voice Diversity Gate

Real referee reports are not uniform. Do not force every report into the same headings, rhythm, or stock phrases. Select distinct referee voices according to the manuscript content, the evidence problems found, and the role of each referee.

## Purpose

Use this gate to avoid repetitive report patterns. Each referee should sound like a different expert, while remaining technically precise, fair, and author-facing.

## Voice-selection rules

- Choose two to four distinct voice profiles for a full report.
- Do not use the same opening structure for all referees.
- Do not use phrases such as "my foremost concern is", "my main concern is", or "I am not convinced" mechanically.
- A referee may write mostly in paragraphs, another may use numbered points, another may provide short page/figure/table comments, and another may write in a more conversational but still professional tone.
- Do not make style variation theatrical. The scientific reasoning must remain coherent and specialist.
- Use conversational distance where appropriate: the referee can say "Here I have a doubt", "I would prefer", "I wonder whether", or "I do not see how", but should avoid sarcasm, hostility, or casual slang.
- Vary minor-comment labels naturally: "Minor comments", "Specific comments", "Some smaller points", "Line- and figure-level comments", or no separate heading when the referee writes embedded comments.

## Author-facing tone

A useful referee can be direct without being cold. Criticism should tell the authors what is unclear, why it matters, and what would make the claim more convincing. If the manuscript is unsuitable for Nature, explain the scientific basis rather than only stating a decision.

## Stock-phrase control

Before finalizing, scan the report. If all referees start with similar sentences, use the same subsection names, or repeat the same motif, rewrite. The report should not look generated from a single template.
