# Micro-Consistency Gate

A Nature-style reviewer should also identify local inconsistencies, wording problems, and context errors that affect clarity or reliability.

Check for:

- inconsistent sample counts;
- inconsistent date ranges;
- inconsistent units;
- inconsistent significance notation;
- inconsistent figure, table, equation, or supplement references;
- missing or inaccessible equations;
- undefined abbreviations;
- repeated words;
- grammar errors that affect meaning;
- non-English residue in an English manuscript;
- mismatch between abstract claims and methods/results;
- mismatch between data availability statements and reproducibility claims;
- mismatch between supplementary material and main-text claims;
- inconsistency between numerical trends and validation uncertainty;
- inconsistent terminology, such as surface nutrient concentration versus whole-estuary nutrient status;
- causal wording unsupported by observational or model-based evidence.

Place most of these issues in a `Minor comments:` section under the relevant referee unless the inconsistency undermines a central claim.

Do not fabricate page, line, figure, or table locations. Use only locations supported by the provided manuscript or extracted text.
