# Editorial Assessment Gate

Nature editors make final decisions, and referees' editorial opinions are advisory. The author-facing referee report should primarily explain who might be interested in the work, what technical failings need to be addressed, and whether the manuscript establishes its claims.

## When to provide a recommendation

Provide an explicit editorial assessment only when one of the following applies:

- the user asks for a decision-level judgement;
- the manuscript has obvious Nature-level novelty, workload, evidence, or methodological failures;
- the manuscript's main claims are not established and no reasonable revision path is apparent from the provided materials;
- the task is framed as a blind test or internal decision-support review.

## Recommendation levels

Use clear but calibrated language:

- `Reject / not suitable for Nature in its present form`: the claim, novelty, evidence, or scope is clearly below the journal threshold.
- `Reject with possible redirection`: the work may be publishable elsewhere but does not support a Nature-level contribution.
- `Major revision before any reconsideration`: the contribution may be important, but central evidence layers are missing or unreliable.
- `Potentially suitable after focused revision`: the central result appears important and the main issues are addressable.

Do not default to a recommendation. If the output is an author-facing peer-review file, place any explicit editorial assessment before the peer-review file or in an internal audit, not inside the referee comments unless the user requests it.

## Fatal concerns

A direct negative recommendation is appropriate when the manuscript has one or more central failures:

- the work lacks a Nature-level conceptual or technical advance;
- the main result is not supported by the data provided;
- validation cannot test the central claim;
- uncertainty is so large or unpropagated that the conclusion is not interpretable;
- causal, policy, or mechanism claims are made without an identification strategy;
- the study is mostly incremental relative to already published work;
- the analysis is not reproducible enough to evaluate the claim.

Even when recommending rejection, retain professional distance and indicate the broad route that would make the work publishable elsewhere.
